  \item If $X$ is a curve of genus $\geq 2$ over a field of characteristic 0, show that the group $\Aut{X}$ of automorphisms of $X$ is finite.

        \begin{proof}
          Suppose $X$ is hyperelliptic, and let $f : X \to \PP^1$ be the unique $g_2^1$ of $X$.
          If $\sigma \in \Aut{X}$, then $f' = f \circ \sigma$ is also a $g_2^1$, so by uniqueness, there exists $\tau \in \Aut{\PP^1}$ such that $f' = \tau \circ f$.
          In particular, $\tau$ sends the ramification points of $f$ to the ramification points of $f'$.
          If $\tau = \id$, then either $\sigma = \id$ or $\sigma$ is the autormophism interchanging the sheets of $f$.
          If $\tau \neq \id$, then $\tau$ gives an element of $S_{2g + 2}$, where $2g + 2$ is the number of ramification points of $f$.

          \fbox{$X$ is not hyperelliptic.} Let $X \subseteq \PP^{g - 1}$ be the canonical embedding of $X$.
        \end{proof}

  \item \textit{Moduli of Curves of Genus 4.} The hyperelliptic curves of genus 4 form an irreducible family of dimension 7.
        The nonhyperelliptic ones form an irreducible family of dimension 9.
        The subset of those having only one $g_3^1$ is an irreducible family of dimension 8.

        \begin{proof}
          Hyperelliptic elliptic curves of genus 4 are determined as two-fold coverings of $\PP^1$, ramified at $0, 1, \infty$, and $2g - 1 = 7$ additional points, up to the action of a certain finite group.
          So the hyperelliptic curves form an irreducible subvariety of dimension 7 of $\goth{M}_4$.

          The canonical embedding of nonhyperelliptic curves of genus 4 is a complete intersection of a unique irreducible quadric surface and an irreducible cubic surface.

          Fix a quadric surface $Q \subseteq \PP^3$.
          The family of all these curves is parametrized by an open set $U \subseteq \PP(\cH^0(Q, \fO_Q(3)))$, which has dimension 15.
          So there is a morphism $U \to \goth{M}_4$ with $F_2 \in U$ mapped to the isomorphism class of the curve $F_2.Q$.

          \fbox{$Q$ is nonsingular.} $U$ has dimension 15.
          $Q.F \cong Q'.F'$ if and only if there exists $\sigma \in \PGL(3)$ such that $\sigma(Q).\sigma(F) = Q'.F'$ if and only if $\sigma(Q) = Q'$ and there exists $\tau \in \PGL(3)$ such that $\tau$ fixes $Q'$ and $\tau(\sigma(F)) = F'$.
          Thus, the fibre of $U \to \goth{M}_4$ over $Q.F$ is the image of the subgroup $G \subseteq \PGL(3)$ fixing $Q$, which has dimension 6.
          Note that $\sigma \in G$ fixing $Q$ is equivalent to fixing a nondegenerate quadratic form.
          If $\sigma \in G$ fixes $Q.F$ as a subscheme of $Q$, then $\sigma$ induces a nontrivial automorphism of $Q.F$.
          The automorphism group of a curve of genus $\geq 2$ is finite.
          Hence, the fibres of $U \to \goth{M}_4$ over $Q.F$ has dimension 6, so the image of $U \to \goth{M}_4$ has dimension 9.

          \fbox{$Q$ is singular.} $U$ has dimension 15, but subgroup $G \subseteq \PGL(3)$ fixing $Q$ has dimension $7$.
        \end{proof}
